## 🎪 KEDER RAIL ON THE SEMI (ships early — HIGH PRIORITY)

**7/9:** Keter track = **64 ft = 8 sticks @ 8 ft** (shipped as ~3 m pieces cut in half → even numbers). Fasteners = **stainless ¼-20 × 1" socket/button-head bolts + washers** (ordered via Amazon; buy locally if late). Yuval must buy **drills + ¼-20 taps + tap handle**, watch a tapping YouTube video, and practice on scrap before doing it ~20× on a ladder 12 ft up. Tap-drill for ¼-20 ≈ #7 / ~3/16" (crew to confirm from a chart).

**7/16 (with Jarrett — the download finally landed):**
- **Location:** rail goes **exactly 2" below the top aluminum casting** (where the roof attaches), between the first two blue rivets. If tight, rest the rail's curved bottom edge on top of a rivet.
- **Tap into the logistics bars** — the W-shaped internal metal (the E-track slots inside); its center flat (~1–1.5" wide) touches the skin. Two side-by-side tapped holes possible at butt joints so both rail ends are supported.
- **CUT the rail so joints land on a support** (crew planned to zip-tie ends — David: must cut). Miter saw + woodworking blade; file/sand burrs.
- **Align slots perfectly** — the bolt rope feeds through; misalignment = misery.
- **End pieces = most critical** (failure starts at ends and zips down). Tap really well; use a longer bolt + backing nut where possible. TT already did **lock washers + backing nuts**; headers above doors can't take backing nuts → tap those.
- **Feeder ends:** file a **45° V-wedge, eased with 80-grit** on *both* ends (feed direction is arbitrary). Butt joints only need alignment, not easing.
- **Bolt rope** = Kevlar fabric bonded around rope. Feed method: tie a rope to the end D-ring, one person on the trailer roof as feeder, others splay the fabric out, one pulls, one prevents kinks → ~5 min. No fish tape needed. Playa dust makes future years harder → hence the eased edges.
- It's sailing / F1-awning tech (Keter serves the boat industry).
